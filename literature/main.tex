\section*{Variational principle}

Consider a membrane with surface velocity $\bm{u}$ immersed in a bulk flow $\bm{U}$. The membrane is area-incompressible, and permeable to the bulk flow with hydraulic conductivity $\beta$; the flux of bulk through the membrane is called $j$, such that the kinematic boundary condition on the surface is
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{u}-\bm{U}=-j\bm{n}.
\end{equation*}
The negative-definite viscosity Laplacian $\bm{L}$ takes $\bm{u}$ to the corresponding surface-viscous forces. The Stokes single-layer operator $\bm{S}$ takes a force distribution to its corresponding disturbance velocity. The membrane is subject to a Helfrich bending energy $\mathcal{W}$ and an osmotic jump of strength $\Gamma$ across the bilayer, such that its free energy is 
\begin{equation*}
    V[\varphi] =\mathcal{W}+\Gamma \mathcal{V}
\end{equation*}
where $\mathcal{V}$ is the enclosed volume. The principle of least energy dissipation states that the dynamics ($\bm{u},\bm{U},j$) will minimize the dissipation and time rate of change of the free energy of the system, subject to external forcing and constraints. We enforce area incompresibility and the kinematic boundary condition via Lagrange multipliers $\gamma$ and $f$ respectively, and write down the variational principle governing our system in primitive variables:
\begin{equation*}
    \min_{\bm{u},\bm{U},j}\max_{\gamma,f}\Big[\big\llangle-\bm{L}\bm{u}|\bm{u}\big\rrangle +\big\llangle\delta_\varphi W|\bm{u}\big\rrangle + \gamma\dot{A} + \frac{1}{2}\big\llangle\bm{U}-\bm{u}^\infty|\bm{S}^{-1}[\bm{U}-\bm{u}^\infty]\big\rrangle+\frac{1}{2\beta}\int_S j^2dA-\Gamma\int_S jdA + \big\llangle\bm{f}|\bm{u}+j\bm{n}-\bm{U}\big\rrangle \Big]
\end{equation*}
where $\llangle\cdot|\cdot\rrangle = \int_S\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle dA$. Stationary with respect to $\bm{U}$ and $\bm{j}$ yields
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{U} = \bm{u}^{\infty}+\bm{S}[\bm{f}]
\end{equation*}
and 
\begin{equation*}
j = \Gamma-\bm{f}\cdot n
\end{equation*}
respectively. Substituting these back into our principle gives
\begin{equation*}
    \min_{\bm{u}}\max_{\gamma,f}\Big[\big\llangle-\bm{L}\bm{u}|\bm{u}\big\rrangle +\big\llangle\delta_\varphi W|\bm{u}\big\rrangle + \gamma\dot{A} + \big\llangle \bm{f}|-\bm{u}+\bm{u}^\infty+\frac{1}{2}\bm{S}[\bm{f}]\big\rrangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_S(\Gamma-\bm{f}\cdot\bm{n})dA\Big]
\end{equation*}
Calling our functional $\mathcal{R}$, stationarity with respect to the remaining variables yields
\begin{align}
\frac{\delta\mathcal{R}}{\delta\bm{f}}&=  -\bm{u}+\bm{u}^\infty+\sd{}S[\bm{f}]-\sd{}\da{}(\Gamma-\bm{f}\cdot \bm{n})\bm{n}=0,\\
\frac{\delta \mathcal{R}}{\delta \bm{u}}&= -\bm{f}-\bm{L}\bm{u}-\bm{f}^b-\bm{f}^\gamma=0, \quad\text{and}\\
\frac{\delta\mathcal{R}}{\delta\gamma} &= \dot{A} = 0.
\end{align}
Respectively, these equations are our boundary integral equation, the Evolving-Stokes equations, and the (global) incompressiblity constraint.
